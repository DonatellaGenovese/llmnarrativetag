# Judge rubrics (draft, 16 November 2026)

Three dimensions, each scored 0–3 (3 = strongly agree, 0 = strongly disagree).
The judge reads the narrative only; for each dimension it gives one or two
sentences of justification, then the score, and quotes the responsible span
for any score of 0 or 1.

## 1. Linguistic Realization (fluency and clarity)

> "The explanation is fluent and clearly written: the language is natural,
> grammatical, and unambiguous."

| score | anchor |
|---|---|
| 3 | Fluent and grammatical throughout; sentences connect smoothly and every statement is unambiguous. |
| 2 | Mostly fluent and clear; minor stiffness or awkward phrasing, nothing that impedes reading. |
| 1 | Noticeably stilted or ambiguous in places; requires rereading. |
| 0 | Disfluent, ungrammatical, or ambiguous enough that meaning is hard to recover. |

Scope: wording, grammar, flow and unambiguity only. The repeated sentence shape
for each observable is imposed by the task and is not penalised.

## 2. Physical Soundness

> "The physical interpretations are correct, and they help a physicist see what
> kind of jet structure lies behind the decision."
> (AD: "… what kind of jet structure makes this jet look anomalous or ordinary.")

| score | anchor |
|---|---|
| 3 | Every physical reading is correct and applied to this jet consistently with whether the narrative says its value is high or low, and the leading observables are tied together into a physical picture of this jet (e.g. a massive jet with three hard prongs) that makes the decision understandable. |
| 2 | Every physical reading is correct, but the readings stay isolated, one observable at a time, and the reader has to assemble the picture; or at most one loose, imprecise phrasing. |
| 1 | One clear physical error, or almost no physical interpretation at all. |
| 0 | Several physical errors, or the physical account of this jet is wrong in its main line. |

Scope: only the physics of the interpretation, judged with standard
jet-substructure knowledge. A clear physical error is one of:

- **High/low reading.** The narrative says a value is high and then describes
  this jet with what low values mean, or the reverse. A general statement such
  as "low values favour two-pronged structure" next to a high value is not an
  error; it is one only when the low-value reading is applied to this jet.
- **Wrong physics for the observable**, e.g. a low tau32 read as two-pronged,
  or constituent multiplicity read as a count of prongs.
- **Overreach.**
  - Top tagging: an interpretation asserted as certain fact about this jet
    ("this jet is a top decaying to bW"), or physics not given in the text
    (mass values, decay mechanisms). Saying that a value is consistent with, or
    typical of, a top decay or QCD radiation is fine.
  - AD: any claim about what the jet is or what produced it (a particle, a
    decay, a process, "signal", new physics), however hedged, and any claim
    about how anomalous jets are distributed. Describing how the jet differs
    from the background reference is fine.

A physical picture must follow the contributions the narrative reports, not
the reader's expectations: when an observable pushes against what its physics
would suggest, saying so plainly is sound; bending the physics to fit the
decision is not. Whether the decision is right is out of scope.

## 3. Overall Satisfaction (helpfulness for understanding the decision)

> "Overall, I am satisfied with this explanation: it helped me understand why
> the model made its decision."
> (AD: "… why the detector found this jet anomalous or ordinary.")

A helpful narrative (a) makes clear which observables drive the decision and in
what order of weight; (b) separates what pushes towards the decision from what
pushes against it (AD: towards anomalous or towards background); (c) conveys
how far the decomposition accounts for the decision (AD: whether the
interpretable model lands in the same band as the detector), and says so
plainly when it accounts for it only partly.

| score | anchor |
|---|---|
| 3 | Delivers (a)–(c): the reader comes away knowing what drove the decision, how strongly, and how far to trust the decomposition. A narrative on a jet the decomposition explains only partly may be brief and lead with that limitation and still score 3. |
| 2 | Conveys the main drivers and their directions, but leaves their relative weight unclear, or never says how far the decomposition accounts for the decision. |
| 1 | Touches the decision but leaves the reasoning thin: drivers not clearly separated by direction or weight, or the decision asserted more than explained. |
| 0 | Gives no meaningful understanding of why the decision was made. |

Scope: understanding of the decision only; physics is dimension 2 and language
is dimension 1. A narrative that flags a weak decomposition honestly is more
satisfying, not less.
